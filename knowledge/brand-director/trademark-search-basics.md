# Trademark basics and the WIPO Global Brand Database for name checks

- Source: Global Brand Database: Frequently Asked Questions — https://www.wipo.int/en/web/global-brand-database/faqs_branddb (accessed 2026-11-28)
- Supporting: Trademarks — https://www.wipo.int/en/web/trademarks (accessed 2026-11-28)
- Publisher: World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO)
- Licence: CC-BY-4.0 (WIPO terms of use default for online content) · Copy in repo: link only

Why this matters for brand-director: the naming method ends with checks the owner must run,
including the WIPO Global Brand Database. Knowing what the database can and cannot show keeps the
naming shortlist honest.

## Key facts: what a trademark is
- A sign that distinguishes one enterprise's goods or services from others'; protected as
  intellectual property.
- Can be words, letters, numerals, drawings, symbols, 3D features (shape, packaging), sounds,
  fragrances, or colour shades used as distinguishing features.
- Protection comes from registration with a national or regional office, for a fee.
- International routes: file in each country, or use WIPO's Madrid System (one application, one
  language, one currency).
- Registration usually lasts ten years and can be renewed indefinitely for further fees.
- It generally gives an exclusive right to use the mark or license it; enforcement is through courts.
- The Nice Classification groups goods and services for registration and helps searching.

## Key facts: the Global Brand Database
- Holds WIPO collections plus those of participating national and regional offices; a coverage list
  is published by WIPO.
- Not complete or instant: national data updates depend on how often each office sends it; the
  Madrid international collection updates daily; EUIPO's TMView data is retrieved daily.
- Searching and accounts are free; export is capped at 180 records per report.
- Search by brand name text, or by logo image ("Find by brand logo": upload jpg, png or webp).
- Uploaded images are confidential and not kept after analysis.
- Saved searches (when logged in) update automatically with new matches.
- WIPO's International Bureau is not mandated to search for third parties, so no result is a
  promise that a name is free.
- It recommends also checking Madrid Monitor and national IP office databases.
- Terms of use forbid bulk or automatic downloading and automatic querying.

## How to apply it here
- In every naming shortlist, add a "Checks to run" column: Global Brand Database text search, logo
  image search for any mark concept, Madrid Monitor, and the national offices for the owner's markets.
- Name the Nice classes likely to matter for a digital product and say they are to be confirmed by
  the owner.
- Record the date of each search the owner runs; results change daily.
- Never automate searches against the database (terms of use); the owner searches by hand.
- An empty result is "no conflict found in this database on this date", not "available".
- Clearance by a qualified trademark lawyer stays the final step; you give general information only.
- Do not use any other company's name, logo or look as a starting point; original assets only.

## Limits
- The FAQ does not describe Boolean operators, Nice-class or status filters, so those features are
  UNVERIFIED here.
- The pages do not explain what makes a mark registrable (distinctiveness, descriptiveness).
- Domain names and social handles are outside the database.
