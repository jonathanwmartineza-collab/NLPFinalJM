# Usability heuristics and laws of UX
Why this matters: the design and product agents judge flows against shared, well-tested principles.

## Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics (summary in our words)
1. Show system status (progress, saving, errors) promptly.
2. Speak the user's language; follow real-world order.
3. Give control and freedom: undo, cancel, easy exits.
4. Be consistent with your product and with platform conventions.
5. Prevent errors (constraints, confirmations for destructive actions, good defaults).
6. Prefer recognition over recall: visible options, examples, remembered input.
7. Support flexibility: shortcuts for experts without hurting novices.
8. Aesthetic, minimalist design: every element competes for attention.
9. Help users recognise, diagnose and recover from errors in plain language.
10. Provide help and documentation where it's needed, task-focused.

## Laws of UX used most
- Fitts's law: important targets big and close to where the pointer/thumb is.
- Hick's law: fewer, grouped choices make decisions faster.
- Jakob's law: users expect your site to work like the others they know.
- Miller's law / chunking: group information into meaningful chunks.
- Doherty threshold: respond within ~400 ms or show progress.
- Aesthetic-usability effect: polish increases perceived usability (but doesn't replace it).
- Peak-end rule: design the best moment and the ending (confirmation, success).

## Severity scale for findings
0 not a problem · 1 cosmetic · 2 minor · 3 major (fix before launch) · 4 catastrophe (blocks a core task)
